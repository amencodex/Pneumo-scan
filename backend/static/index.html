Pneumonia basics

What is pneumonia?

Pneumonia is a lung infection that can make breathing harder because the tiny air sacs in the lungs may fill with fluid, mucus, or pus.

Simple explanation

It affects the air sacs where oxygen enters the blood.

When pneumonia develops, parts of the lung can become inflamed. This can make oxygen exchange less efficient, which is why people may feel short of breath, tired, feverish, or have a cough. The cause can be bacterial, viral, fungal, or related to aspiration.

  • 1Common signs: cough, fever, chills, chest discomfort, fast breathing, and fatigue.
  • 2Higher risk: young children, older adults, smokers, and people with weak immunity or chronic illness.
  • 3Medical review matters: symptoms, oxygen level, exam findings, labs, and X-rays are reviewed together.
Safety first

When should someone seek urgent care?

A website or AI model cannot diagnose pneumonia. If symptoms are serious or getting worse, a clinician should review the patient quickly.

  • !Urgent symptoms: trouble breathing, blue lips, confusion, severe chest pain, or very low energy.
  • !Watch closely: high fever, dehydration, worsening cough, or oxygen levels lower than normal.
  • !For this project: X-ray AI is support only; final decisions should stay with qualified medical staff.

Causes and risk

What can cause pneumonia?

Pneumonia is an infection of the lungs. The air sacs can fill with fluid or pus, making breathing harder.

Bacteria

Common bacterial causes include pneumococcus. Bacterial pneumonia may require antibiotics, chosen by a clinician.

Viruses

Respiratory viruses can cause pneumonia, including RSV and influenza. Viral cases need different care than bacterial cases.

Fungi and risk factors

Fungal pneumonia is more likely in people with weakened immune systems. Risk rises with age, lung disease, malnutrition, smoking, and chronic illness.

Prevention

How pneumonia risk can be reduced

Prevention is not one thing. It combines vaccines, nutrition, clean air, hygiene, and early access to care.

Protective steps

  • Vaccination, including pneumococcal, Hib, influenza, and other recommended vaccines.
  • Good nutrition, breastfeeding support for infants, and treatment of malnutrition.
  • Hand hygiene, cleaner household air, and avoiding tobacco smoke exposure.
  • Early care-seeking for fast breathing, chest indrawing, fever, low oxygen, or severe cough.

Why imaging helps

Chest X-rays can help clinicians assess lung opacity patterns, but they are only one part of the diagnosis. Local model projects should support learning, triage research, or workflow experiments, not replace clinicians.
